# Detector provenance

Authoritative record of which data trained which detector version. Check this
before adding a log to any training set, and before choosing a holdout.

**Rule: never train a detector on a log that is a holdout for a downstream
consumer.** Volumes appear under several names (see Volume identity), so match
on volume identity, not directory name.

## Volume identity (aliases for the same physical scan)

`/mnt/D/datasets/ct_log/CT/` and `/mnt/D/datasets/ct_log/375492_SM_2025/` hold
the same volumes under different names. Verified by pixel comparison:

| raw dir | staging / Supervisely log | note |
|---|---|---|
| `CT/01` | log `1` | bit-identical |
| `CT/02` | log `2` | same volume |
| `CT/03` | log `3` | same volume |
| `CT/04` | log `4` | bit-identical |
| `CT/10` | log `10` | pixel-identical (re-encoded, so `cmp` differs; corr = 1.0000) |

There is no `CT/1`, `CT/2`, `CT/3`, `CT/4`. Logs `05`, `06`, `08`, `09` use the
same name in both trees. Everything from `CT/11` upward was unannotated raw at
the time of writing.

Consequence: picking training logs by directory name from the "unannotated" raw
pool will silently pull in logs 1, 2, 3, 4, 10 via their `CT/0N` aliases.

### `yolo11n_obb_2cls_v4` (2026-09-20)

Dataset `ann_pipeline/out/knot_yolo_obb_2cls_v4`, built by

| | median | p25 | p75 |
|---|---|---|---|
| human GT | 12.9 px | 10.5 | 17.9 |
| v3 | 16.1 px | 13.4 | 19.6 |
| v4 | 14.6 px | 11.4 | 18.8 |

v4 closes roughly half the gap to human (1.25x -> 1.13x inflation).

**It did not fix the thin tail**, and the shortfall is far worse than npz-stage

## Known downstream holdouts

The DINOv3 segmentation model (`src/train_kwp.py`) uses:

- val: logs 1, 4
- test: log 10

Its training set is auto-annotated logs generated by `run_obb_only.py`. Those
generated logs must exclude logs 1, 4, 10 and their `CT/01`, `CT/04`, `CT/10`
aliases.

Note the tension: detector v4 trains on logs 1 and 4, which are segmentation
val logs. This is acceptable only because the segmentation model consumes
detector *output* on other logs, never the detector's own training frames — but
segmentation metrics on logs 1 and 4 are therefore not fully independent of
detector fitting. Log 10 is clean for both: held out of detector v3 and v4
training, and is the segmentation test log.

## Diagnostics worth remembering

- Knot mask thickness in anchor-free output comes from the predicted OBB width,
  not from MedSAM2 propagation. Measured on log 05: ellipse seed 11.8 px ->
  propagated output 12.1 px. On human log 4, GT mask thickness 10.4 px vs
  `minAreaRect` minor side 10.1 px (0.97x), so the rasterization is faithful.
- Raising detection confidence makes boxes *thicker*, not tighter, and roughly
  halves detections (log 05: 12.0 px at conf 0.40 -> 18.2 px at conf 0.70).
  High-confidence detections are the large obvious knots. Keep conf = 0.40.
- **Median knot thickness is the wrong statistic for comparing auto vs human
  labels.** Measured at full density (every annotated frame, n = 826-969
  pooled), excluding log 3: human median 14.0 px vs auto 15.8 px — only 1.13x,
  essentially parity. Earlier claims of 1.5x / 1.31x / 1.25x were all strided
  median comparisons and were stride-sensitive.
- **The real defect is a missing thin tail.** Fraction of knots thinner than a
  threshold, human vs auto (log 3 excluded):

  | threshold | human | auto |
  |---|---|---|
  | < 8 px | 28.6% | 10.7% |
  | < 10 px | 35.9% | 16.1% |
  | < 12 px | 44.1% | 34.7% |
  | < 15 px | 53.4% | 46.6% |

  Per-log `p25` shows it directly: human p25 ranges 5.7-11.3 px, auto p25 is
  pinned at 10.0 px on every auto log. The pipeline almost never emits a knot
  thinner than ~10 px.
### Measure delivered labels, not npz

**Knot geometry measured from `(pred == 2)` in the propagation npz is not a
valid proxy for the labels a downstream model consumes.** `build_auto_annotation`
applies several rejection filters afterwards — 150 px minimum area,
pith-proximity exclusion (< 25 px), eccentricity < 0.7, solidity < 0.85, and
clipping to `wood_final` — and thin knots are the most likely to be rejected.

Log 13, v4 output, same source data at two stages:

| stage | instances | median | p25 | frac < 8 px |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| npz `(pred == 2)` | 202 | 17.1 px | 10.5 | 12.4% |
| delivered ann JSON | 120 | 17.1 px | 12.1 | 4.2% |
| human reference | — | 14.0 px | — | 28.6% |

Filtering removes ~40% of instances and two thirds of the thin ones. The real
shortfall in delivered labels is ~7x (28.6% -> ~4%), not the ~2.3x the npz
stage suggests.

**The two stages also rank logs differently**, so screening on npz is actively
misleading. Post-filter across the first three v4 logs:

| log | instances | median | p25 | frac < 8 px | (npz frac < 8 px) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | 120 | 17.1 px | 12.1 | 4.2% | 12.4% |
| 14 | 212 | 19.7 px | 13.3 | 0.9% | 10.5% |
| 15 | 134 | 17.9 px | 11.3 | 4.5% | 22.5% |

Log 15 looked best at the npz stage and is mid-pack delivered; log 14 looked
mid-pack and is the worst delivered. Always measure from the converted
annotations.

- For what actually removes thin knots, see "Filter ablation" below — it was
  measured, not guessed.
- Per-log variance is high in *both* populations: human log 4 is 10.8 px while
  human log 1 is 14.1 px; auto log 09 (12.6 px) sits inside the human range
  while auto log 3 (34.4 px) is a far outlier. "Auto knots are fatter than
  human knots" is not true at the log level.

### Filter ablation: what removes thin knots

Replayed the `build_auto_annotation` filter chain over v4 npz output for logs
13-16 (every 3rd frame), attributing each rejected instance to the first filter
that rejected it. 543 instances entered the filters, 274 of them thin (< 8 px).

| filter | rejected | of which thin | share of thin losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| `area < 150` | 227 (41.8%) | 224 | 84% |
| `solidity < 0.85` | 63 (11.6%) | 43 | 16% |
| `eccentricity < 0.7` | 34 (6.3%) | 0 | 0% |
| kept | 219 (40.3%) | 7 | — |

Only 7 of 274 thin instances survive — a 97% rejection rate at the thin end.

**`eccentricity < 0.7` contributes nothing to the thin-knot deficit.** The
threshold rejects eccentricity *below* 0.7, i.e. round blobs; a thin streak is
high-eccentricity by construction and passes trivially. Leave it alone.

**Area is the dominant rejector (84%), solidity a real secondary (16%).** An
earlier single-log estimate put this at 53/47; at ~10x the sample it is 84/16.
Note the chain is ordered — area runs first and claims instances solidity would
otherwise catch — so these shares are "first filter to reject", not independent
contributions.

Proposed v2 experiment: 2x2 over area (150 / 40) x solidity (0.85 / 0.6),
eccentricity untouched. Check whether the recovered thin instances are real
knots or propagation noise before adopting.

## Downstream result: segmentation v1-extended (2026-09-21)

The DINOv3 segmentation model was retrained on 20 auto-annotated logs (6 older
Phase2 logs + the 14 v4-generated logs), val = human logs 1 and 4, test =
human log 10. Reported by the segmentation session; see `SEGMENTATION.md`
(commit 18e7e1e).

Held-out log 10, best checkpoint (~epoch 12):

| class | before (7 logs, v3 detector) | after (20 logs, v4 detector) |
|---|---|---|
| wood | 0.958 | 0.966 |
| knot | 0.356 | 0.390 |
| pith (seg) | 0.158 | 0.236 |
| fg | 0.490 | 0.531 |
| pith regression median | 12.1 px | 5.2 px |

Train-val gap at epoch 10 was +0.137 vs +0.204 on the 7-log set, so the extra
data reduced overfitting.

**Treat this as a floor, not a converged result:**

- The run never finished. It crashed three times (rc=132 SIGILL after 6.4 h,
  then rc=1, then a third start where the supervisor itself died so auto-resume
  never fired), stopping mid-epoch 16 on 2026-09-21 10:02. Resumable from
  ~epoch 15 via `kwp_v1_extended.resume.pth`.
- The earlier 7-log baseline peaked at epoch 23, so ~16 epochs is short.
- Three variables changed at once — detector v3 -> v4, 6 -> 20 logs, and log
  composition — so the gain is not attributable to any single change.
- The labels still carry the ~10 px thin-knot floor (see Filter ablation), so
  knot at 0.390 is measured against training data missing 97% of thin knots.

  That gap is boundary localisation from its coarse decoder.
